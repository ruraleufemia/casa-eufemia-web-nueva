La Mancha food: wine, cheese, olive oil, pistachios and tapas

What you cannot leave without trying, and where, from someone who grew up here.

Nobody should leave Casa Rural Eufemia without tasting La Mancha. Wine, cheese, olive oil, pistachios and hearty traditional cooking: the best way to discover it is a tapas crawl, bar to bar, as we do here.

Local produce

  • Wine: La Mancha is vineyard country, and Tomelloso is a town of wineries and caves. Ask for the local wine in any bar.
  • Manchego cheese: made from Manchega sheep's milk, cured or semi-cured, as a tapa or with good bread.
  • Olive oil: from the local mills, for dipping bread or dressing a pisto.
  • Pistachios: more and more fields in La Mancha grow pistachios. Try them roasted with your drink.

Dishes you have to try

  • Gachas manchegas: the classic winter dish.
  • Galianos: a shepherd's stew with flat unleavened bread, typical of the area.
  • Caldereta: a slow-cooked lamb stew.
  • Migas and pisto manchego: simple and full of flavour.

Tapas route: Silvia's favourite places

The best way to discover La Mancha food is a tapas route: a tapa often comes with every glass of wine. Go from bar to bar and try a bit of everything.

  • Mercado de Abastos, Tomelloso: the old market, renovated as a food hall, in the style of Madrid's Mercado de San Miguel but smaller. Several stalls and bars: some sell food, others drinks or fish, and you can pick products that they cook for you on the spot.
  • La Antigua Restaurante & Bar (Tomelloso): in an old Castilian house near the bullring, with a bar for tapas and a good selection of local wines.
  • El Rinconcito (Tomelloso): on the Plaza de España, right in the centre. A great place to taste excellent wines.
  • Alhambra (Tomelloso): recently renovated and excellent, for a set menu or a treat of roast shoulder of lamb or lamb's liver. Very good food and very good wines.
  • Cueva La Martina (Campo de Criptana): a 16th-century cave house carved into the windmill hill. A spectacular place for a glass of wine, next to the windmills.
  • The pool bar in Arenales: the village's only bar, in Los Pinares next to the playground. Huge sandwiches at good prices and lovely sunsets. Great in summer.
Silvia's tipAnyone who comes to Casa Rural Eufemia should leave having at least seen the windmills of Campo de Criptana, gone down into a Tomelloso wine cave and done a tapas route tasting our wines, cheeses, olive oils and pistachios.

For a special occasion

  • Las Musas (Campo de Criptana): another of Silvia's favourites, La Mancha cooking next to the windmills, with a Michelin Guide Bib Gourmand.
  • Epílogo (Tomelloso): one Michelin star, La Mancha cuisine reinvented.
  • Marquinetti (Tomelloso): pizzas by Jesús Marquina, several times world champion.

And at home

Buy cheese, wine and olive oil in the nearby villages and make your own aperitivo in the courtyard, or light the barbecue and the wood-fired oven. If you stay 5 nights or more booking directly, you can choose a wine tasting with me or lamb from the wood oven as your free experience.

More about food, wineries and routes on the official tourism website, Visita Tomelloso. The Tourist Office is in the Posada de los Portales, on the Plaza de España.
